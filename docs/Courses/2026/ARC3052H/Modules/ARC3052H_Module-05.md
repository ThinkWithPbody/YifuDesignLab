### Module-05 Small Firms, Big Responsibilities: Independent Practice Models (KCA) + CCDC 2 (Builder–Owner Contracts)

Small residential practice
[[Wiki/KCA|Wiki/KCA]]

- Background
	- Toronto
	- UofT for art history
	- American MARCH
	- KPMB before MARCH
	- Then Europe
	- Come back for family worked with Shim
	- Demanding work so worked on her own
	- Specialize in single family residential projects
		- Office of 3
		- Easiest as a small practice 
		- Don’t take more than couple of years
	- Some projects they are developers
		- Buy properties, permits, etc
- Getting work
	- Where do clients come from?
	- Connections | Networking
		- A firm that can’t do too small of a project
		- A friend needing a project done 
		- Referrals from
			- Builders
			- Friends
			- Past clients
	- Repeat business
	- Print | Social Media | Marketing
	- Construction Site Signage
	- Awards
	- Open Competitions
		- very few for residential
	- OAA Directory
- Securing the project
	- Out of 10 leads, 1 will turn into a project
- Ask questionable about the project
	- Ask every client to write a project brief, a mission statement
		- Site
		- Goals
		- Scope
		- Priorities
		- Proposals are written based on this
	- Project relationships can be complicated
		- Client and sponsor may be different
	- always try to educate the client what architects do
		- Any project under 6000 ft2 you don’t need an licensed architect as long as you have Building Code Exam number BCI
	- Example
		- Ask
			- Jobs (not income)
			- Family size
			- Previous experience with design, building
			- Decision making style
		- Always think about interviewing them as well, either with clients or working for other architects
			- Every project you’ll learn
			- The more you learn, the more you’ll trust your instinct
			- There’ll always be another project
			- When it doesn’t go well or not aligned, it’s really hard
- Discuss time and budget
	- 8-12 months design
	- 12-18 months construction
	- Cost per square foot (the most important
		- [[../../../../../Diarmuid Nash|Diarmuid Nash]] 850-900 a little over budget for mass timber
		- Costing usually before anything happening at all so estimated based on past project
		- Reconcile client budget with estate
		- [[Wiki/KCA|Wiki/KCA]] First project was with builder
			- Find a builders understands what you’re looking for, who appreciate your existence and want your involvement
			- Not ones that want you to disappear and do crappy work and have it done so you can’t do anything about it
	- Fees are based on (make sure you’re charging enough)
		- Required size, complexity, and quality 
		- Location of the architect and project
		- Level of service (from planning drawings to a full design service)
		- Information available (brief feasibility of project and site conditions)
		- Level of experience of client
		- Compatibility with the experience of the practice
	- Types of fee
		- Percentage of build cost
		- Lump sum
		- Hourly rate
			- [[Wiki/KCA|Wiki/KCA]] uses hourly rate
			- Sounds scary
			- The hours spent on the project depends on the client
			- Range from 140-275
				- Hourly book keeper
					- Track your hours spend on each project
					- End of month, tally by book keeper and review
				- When invoicing, can’t actually charge all of hours
				- if 250 hours on schematic, might run out of hours when design isn’t done
				- If you require approval services, extra hours
			- Profit is important to keep business running long term to counter uncertainty
		- Cost +
			- Cost of labour + overhead
			- Any fixed fee on construction always end up getting bigger
			- The better something is coordinated, the better drawings are detailed and communicated, the smoother everything runs, lowering construction cost overrun
- Contract
	- OAA 4 pages standard form of contract for architect’s services
	- Consultants
		- Landscape
			- Elise Shelby
			- usually done in house
- Running the project 
	- Deposit
		- 10,000 
			- If the project is 80,000, this should be reasonable
			- Smaller projects might be less
			- need them to be invested
			- Bill every month
			- Don’t give the money back until the end where they are credited
		- Similar to lawyers
			- Pay 4000 to get them on case
			- They credit against it when calculating fees
	- Phases 
		- Pre-design
			- Frame project
			- Get topography survey
				- Trees
					- Application to injure or destroy a tree
			- Permits where you can
		- Schematic
			- Basic Drawings on front facade, plans, interior elevations
			- no idea about materials
			- General idea of programs, size
			- Cost
			- Ask client to sign to approve the scheme
		- Design Development
			- Drawings bigger scale
			- Type of stair, materials, construction
			- Facade decelopment
			- Supplies in, returns
			- Bring in mechanical designer and structural engineer
			- Cost again
		- Construction Drawings 
			- Costs are verified twice across builders, should be the same
				- We don’t like clients bringing their own builders, they might be bad at pricing or just low balling for jobs 
				- Ends up being harder for architects
			- Drawings at half inch scale
				- ~1:25
			- Cost again
			- Get permit 
			- Start construction 
		- Construction Specifications
			- You have to be there
				- Constantly think about every single detail 
			- Show drawings review
				- Work with steel, concrete, all your trades
			- Important to work with people that understand your intent and you trust! 
				- when she left Shim, Joe Lobko who was leaving work for another introduced two clients and recommended a builder 
				- Once you find them, you want to keep nurturing that relationship as much as you can
			- Site supervisor could be important
			- Work with photographer too
		- Construction Administration 
		- Building Occupancy
- Approvals
	- Zoning variance
		- Neighbors within 250 ft will know what you’re doing and they have a chance to object in hearing
		- You want to knock on their door, tell them first and involve them in the process
	- Possible stakeholders
		- Urban forestry
		- Transportation
		- Heritage
			- Even if it’s all interior
		- Ravine conservation
			- 10m back from the top of your bank
		- Planning Department
			- Everything will be reviewed
			- Good to be proactive, call all of them in the beginning of the project
		- Fire department
		- Engineering and construction services
- Takeaways
	- Listen well and understand your client
	- Your role involves managing a complex process and multiple players
	- Develop fees based on realistic
