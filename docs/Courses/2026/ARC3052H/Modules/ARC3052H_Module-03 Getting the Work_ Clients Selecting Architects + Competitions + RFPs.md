### Module-03 Getting the Work: Clients Selecting Architects + Competitions + RFPs

### Innovation wins - Design competitions in Canada today By [[People/Joe Lobko|Joe Lobko]],[[Wiki/OAA|Wiki/OAA]], [[Wiki/FRAIC|Wiki/FRAIC]]

- On procurement methodology
	- How do you analyze the competition beyond just what the programs specify
	- OAA Design competition
		- Reflects a theme about Clint’s looking for innovative projects to represent their values and ambitions
		- Toronto city hall - at the time largest design competition in the world
	- Experience with competitions
		- Sharing of knowledge and experience
	- Competitions are opportunities for innovation and investment 
	- Ideas competition
		- Exploring ideas together and practice
	- Fort York Visitor Centre two stage design 2006
	- TSA Ideas competitions for Design of the Toronto Waterfront 2000
		- how do you ensure a competition is ran fairly
	- TCHX Regent Park Affordable Housing Design Competition 2005
	- Green Line Hydro Corridor Competition 2012
	- The Electric Fueling Station of the Future 2021
- Recipe for a successful Competition
	1. An ambition client/sponsor with a special project in mind
		- No money often leads to no good submissions
	2. A budget adequate for both project implementation and competition process
	3. A thorough and thoughtful building program with aspirations plainly described
	4. An available and suitable site with design parameters clearly defined
	5. Clearly stated project priorities that inform the selection criteria identified
	6. A diverse, most excellent, informed, and capable jury
	7. A public exhibition/presentation process that can both inspire and inform
	8. A technical support team capable of delivering an appropriate project brief and a concise critique of each submission received for the jury’s consideration
	9. Design teams that are motivated, resourced and capable
	10. An effective, independent Professional Advisor
- Competition submission keys (how do you do all this in 8 weeks)
	- 3 weeks in have core ideas on paper, to allow confidential meetings on advice
	- Articulate ideas clearly with diagrams (slides)
	- Dedicated competition team
	- great flexibility and potential 
	- Communities benefit all 4 seasons
	- Adhere to competition briefs and anything risky should be able to be removed and skill satisfy requirements
	- Meet performance targets
	- Linking cladding and energy makes no sense - building needs to continually invest in cutting edge technology
	- Power of a good realistic cross-section with diagrams explaining core systems and techniques 
	- Multiple objectives, “capacity”, the project will evolve and grow after winning, it’s the beginning of the design process
	- Know your jury and Jury Chair
	- Be prepared to present publicly and be educational and inspirational


### The Role of marketing in Architecture By Katie, NSCAD

- RFP
	- Very intense, 2-4 weeks and can come at any time
	- 95% of work is from RFP
	- Form teams before RFP so you’re saving time and a little ahead of the process
- Ultimate goal of marketing
	- Get new work
- Marketing team
	- 4 person team
	- 2 proposals lead
	- 1 website, media
- key activities
	- Social media
	- Website
	- Award submissions
	- …
	- Including hoarding signage on the job site
	- Press / PR
		- [[../../../../../Donald Schmitt|Donald Schmitt]] and Ontario Place Spa exposure
- Firm logo
- bids&tenders RFP
	- Need to open up and check qualifications 
- Types of Tender Documents
- Process
	- EOI
	- RFQ
	- Shortlist
	- RFP
	- Bidders conference
	- Review / score / interview / negotiation
	- Award / debrief
- Go/no go form
	- Constantly evaluate 
		- Odds of winning?
		- Can you meet requirements
		- Is it a sector you specialize in
		- Is budget within threshold
		- Do you and marketing team have capacity to complete the proposal
		- …
- Technical Accuracy
	- RFP very detailed and have to comply with every item
	- Essentially Contract A the first contract
	- They are looking for any reason to disqualify you from a pile of RFPs
		- Accurate technical responses
		- Typeface, sizes, page count
		- Forms
		- Deadlines
			- If you don’t make a deadlines you lose everyone all consultants’ time
	- Reference timeline slide
- Putting together a team (sub-consultants)
	- If you’re adding an elevator consultant and no one else is, you are at disadvantage
	- Submit Clarification request so everyone is on level playing field
	- sub-consultants legally must receive every part of the RFP and addendums  even if unrelated
		- That they can’t later say “you didn’t include it and now I have to add fee”
		- Ensure all scope is covered
		- No scope overlap
		- Format all of their resumes so the proposal looks like a cohesive team
	- Often go to 3 sub-consultants for each discipline to compare fees or negotiate fees
- Writing & content
	- Know your client!
		- Read everything about the client so you can tell them you are the team
	- Tell the story
	- research / keywords
	- Tailor boilerplate pieces
	- Bespoke approach
	- …
- Using AI
	- Assist in keeping all the proposal documents straight
	- Clean up writing
	- There’s evidence that clients are using AI for RFP too because they ask for EVERYTHING
- Graphic design & layout
	- Research client brand design and use it!
	- …
- Fees
	- Even if the fees score is only 10%, everyone on the short list will score similarly on the other 90%, and the 10% matters because they want the best design for the lest money
- Time Task Matrix
	- Detailed time sheet of everyone on the team
	- Diabolical! Everyone hates those
	- The product that we sell is hours
- Making it to the shortlist / interview
	- Usually 30%
	- Recently 20%
	- MTA made it 50% last year
	- know who’s in the room
	- Review RFA RFP
	- Practise practise!
	- This team is always looking for a connection, your tone of voice etc.
	- Questions and answers are where you win or lose them
		- The informal sense of relationship
	- Anticipate questions is more important than answering questions
	- 11x17 face name print so they know who you are
	- Celebrate, Marketing project sheets and press release, or debrief if you lost
- Everyone in the team should
	- Keep CV updated
	- LinkedIn updated
	- Follow firm’s social media and share
	- Contribute to pursuits as needed
	- Follow sales process and know your role
	- Collectively, we’re all representing the firm’s brand
	- Talk to clients
	- **What do you do? One sentence elevator pitch for yourself and the firm**
- smps, CPSM
	- Certified Professional Services Marketor
- Networking
	- Add everyone to LinkedIn and keep in touch
	- It’s all about relationships
	- Network vertically as much as you can horizontally
	- TSA (everyone should be a member, great organization!)
	- OAA
	- Other organizations

### Quiz

1. **In architectural practice, marketing is best understood as:** A strategic process of positioning, relationships, and communicating value
2. **Which of the following is an example of “relationship-based marketing” for architects?** Maintaining ongoing connections with past clients and collaborators
3. **A strong marketing message in architecture should primarily communicate:** The firm’s value to the client and what problems it solves
4. **Succession planning is best defined as**: The strategic process for transferring ownership, leadership, and knowledge
5. **Which is a major risk of not having a succession plan?** Abrupt closure and loss of clients
6. **The lecture suggests that a succession plan often takes**: 5–10 years
7. **Design competitions are typically used for:** Important civic projects and special sites
8. **Which of the following is listed as one of the “10 essential ingredients” of a successful design competition?** A diverse, informed, capable jury
9. **According to the lecture, why is a competition process often viewed as “suspect” if there is no Professional Advisor?** The fairness of the process and result is questioned
10. **The jury commentary for the Etobicoke Civic Centre 1st place submission emphasized that sustainability was:** Deep within the “DNA of the design”
