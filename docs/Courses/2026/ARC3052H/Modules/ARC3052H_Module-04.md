- Putting a practise back on the map
	- The devil is in the details
	- 5 years before invited to be a partner
	- Ownership based on an investment derived from the annual operating costs
	- Share in firms profits or losses accordingly
	- 10% share, salary is an advance in assumed profits
	- Your partners are your ship mates
	- it’s high risk
		- Valuation is an art rather than a science
		- Succession is a wild card
	- He was the last person among partners wanting to retire to invest in the partnership
		- Asked around
	- Attracted An invitation from a global lractice - Perkins&Will
		- Was looking for good portfolio, institutional projects, young leadership
		- For 8x average NSR over past 8 years plus “goodwill”
		- Joined and worked together on interior and residential, now interdisciplinary and cross sector
	- Evaluated the value of the firm and every partner got cash
	- Everyone who was a partner could choose to buy new Perkins&Will shares (privately held)

- Financial Planning and Reporting
	- The fine art of sales, NSR, raw labour, overhead, multiplier (overhead ratio, in Toronto 2.8, already high but higher in New York and higher in London), profit, chargeability, productivity
	- ![[./Attachments/ARC3052H_Module-04/ARC3052H_Module-04_IMG_5577.jpeg|ARC3052H_Module-04_IMG_5577.jpeg]]
	- Always looking at Office Earnings Analysis By Project
	- Even bottom of the pack, still twice as profitable as other companies because they understood where they were going
	- Studio chargeability always examined
		- Younger might be 80% chargeable
		- He’s 50% chargeable 
		- Other 50% winning work, building relationships
		- if chargeability is low, profit is low
		- If 3 people work at 50%, Better to have 2
	- OpenDEAR Meta Portfolio
		- Annually, Offices submit photographed projects and gets graded by management according to their framework
		- how well is it detailed and crafted, sustainability, etc.
		- Always get stronger between design and business performance
