### Module-07 Who Backs the Project: Surety Bonds + Collaborative Practice Models (SOCA)

Surety Bonds  
By Sonny Ingram

- when personalities don’t get along with each other
    - Poisonous relationships
    - Owner fired builders without telling anyone
        - Or builders going bankrupt, dissolved
        - Large builders could dissolve just as easily
    - but projects could complete, albeit with delay, because of surety bonds
- Surety Bonds are for the benefit of the owner
    - Is a deed (a document in writing on paper signed sealed and delivered (now could be electronic acceptance)) whereby one person undertakes to pay a specified sum of money to another, either immediately or at a future date
- Main types of construction bonds
    - Bid bonds
    - Performance bonds
    - Labour and material payment bonds
    - Lien bonds
- Construction Act
    - Most commonly
        - Performance bond
        - Labour and material payment bond
    - Prescribed claims procedure set out in statutory bonds
- The surety obligation
    - Most surety are placed in insurance companies
    - But they are not insurance
        - 2 way insurance (typically dealing with negligent work), vs
        - 3 party relationship (on execution of the contract)
            - Obligee (owner), Principle (general contractor), Surety (bonding company)
        - on default vs on demand
        - Undertaking to pay
        - Condition, the performance of which nullifies the undertaking to pay
        - The bond is not to the builder but to the contract itself
            - Bonds are project specific security
    - The underlying contract
        - Principal’s obligation primary
        - Surety’s obligation secondary (guarantee)
- The structure of the surety relationship
    - Chart…
    - the surety is truly on the side line, has no control of anything
    - The amount left to be paid to the insolvent builders is always less than what it costs to boot project back up again, which places owner at great vulnerability
    - Why would it take on such risks?
- Bid Bonds
    - The delta between dropping first bid and second bidder would be paid by surety, or sometimes 10% of bid
- Performance Bonds
    - A guarantee by the surety to the obligee (owner or GC) that the principal (GC or SC) will perform a specific construction contract, subject to certain conditions set out in the face of the bond
    - current architecture landscape: 60-70 million jobs don’t exist anymore, it’s either big (science centre 1 billion) or small
- Performance Bond Claims
    - 3 Conditions for a claim
        - Principal in default
        - Principal declared to be in default
        - Obligee has performances it’s obligations
            - if owner held on to payments, the claims bond when at breach of contract, the claim is challenged
    - The Obligee’s obligations
        - The Surety’s right to notice from the Obligee
        - The Surety’s right to investigate
    - Claims Process
- The Surety’s acceptance of Liability
    - Surety’s options under the bond
        - Remedy the default
        - Complete the contract in accordance with its terms and conditions
        - Obtain a bid or bids for submission to the Obligee for completing the contract (most likely this)
    - Surety’s unwritten option ($)
    - Eglinton Crosstown LRT goes through dispute and court decisions and took 15 years for completion
        - Which is why incorporated (IPD) are being favoured by public projects, because all parties sign the same contract and no builder has monopoly on a contract
- The Surety’s denial of Liability
    - Misrepresentation
        - Lying
        - That low bid is a fraud and we would not have taken on the bond if we knew!
    - No default by Principal
    - Failure to notify
        - 4 year contract altered to a 7 year contract without notifying the Surety
        - That’s a much riskier contract we could not bond!
    - Variation of bonded contract
    - Impossibility
        - Covid - late is not impossible
        - An impossible design that can’t be build
    - Limitations
- Labour and Material Payment Bonds
    - The Surety guarantees that the principal will pay the amount of claimants who are unpaid by the principal, subject to conditions of the L&M bond
    - The Surety’s obligations under L&M bonds is completely separate from its obligations under the performance bond
- Lien Bonds
    - Financial guarantee to the accountant of the court
    - Stands in place of land
    - Claims process
    - Lien is critical and very impactful because construction will stop if payments stop
    - Two ways to provide security for Lien
        - Cash paid in court, with additional security
        - Lien bond
    - A lien is incredible leverage
        - Hurts reputation
        - Doesn’t easily go away
        - 100% will be a claim at the end of the job
        - A Lien Bond ensures that regardless of the fight and what’s going on, all of that is to the side and the project could continue
- The Indemnity Agreement
    - Entitlement to indemnification
    - Entitlement to access to books and records
    - Entitlement to financial information
    - Entitlement to assignments of
        - Materials
        - Equipment
        - Subcontracts
        - Contract balances
- Do’s and Dont’s
    - …
    - Don’t waive contract provisions, don’t allow schedule to slip and keep paying
    - Don’t get talked into certifying work that isn’t complete. Material not on site, don’t certify
    - Don’t make big changes to contracts and always notify Surety
    - All nuance - no white, no black, all grey, very easy to allow humanity of it all to get in the way
